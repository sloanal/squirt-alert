How the model works

Sperm vs. fluid. Sperm are made in the testes and stored in the epididymis. More than 90% of the fluid comes from the seminal vesicles and prostate. The two refill at different speeds, so Reserve tracks them separately.

Sperm. By default a full reserve is about 250 million per ejaculate, roughly the typical total count after 2–7 days of abstinence. A release empties about 60% of the stored sperm. The store then refills slowly, with a time constant of about 3 days: around 57% after 1 day, 78% after 3 days and 94% after 7 days. Sperm count keeps rising with abstinence for longer than volume does.

Volume. The default is 3.0 mL, the average for men aged 40–44. A release drains about 90% of the fluid. It refills with a time constant of about 1.6 days, consistent with seminal-vesicle MRI studies.

Fertility window. In studies, DNA fragmentation (damage to sperm DNA) is lowest after 1–2 days of abstinence and rises with longer gaps. Motility (how well sperm swim) drops after more than 7 days. Releases less than 24 hours apart may cost a little quality. Reserve therefore marks 1–4 days as prime, 4–7 days as good and 7+ days as stale. The WHO recommends 2–7 days before a semen test and ESHRE suggests 3–4. When trying to conceive, sex every 1–2 days in the fertile window is the usual advice.

These are population averages, not your numbers. A semen analysis gives you a real baseline. This is not a medical device.
